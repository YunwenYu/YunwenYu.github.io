The 10% Myth: A Brain-Sized Lie
The 10% myth is alluring; it opens up a world of possibilities… well, that's bad news for movie plots and motivational speakers—the idea that “we only use 10% of our brain” is as scientifically unreliable as a horoscope printed on a bubble gum wrapper. The reality is that our brain is a complex machine that needs all its parts to function properly in order to function properly and healthily.
Totally unreliable : )
Where Did This Nonsense Come From?
No one knows exactly who started this myth, but it likely began as a misinterpretation of early neurological research. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, scientists noted that not all neurons fired at once — and somehow, people twisted that into “we only use 10% of our brains.” It spread like gossip at a high school cafeteria, and soon even famous people were quoting it. Hollywood didn't help either — movies like Lucy and Limitless built entire plots on the idea that a secret, unused portion of our brain could turn us into superhumans.
Spoiler alert: that's not how brains (or science) work.
You don't need 90% of unused brain space to be fascinating. The brain you already have is wild, just like a circus performer who never sleeps.

What Science Actually Says
Modern neuroscience shows that we use virtually every part of our brain, and most of it is active almost all the time — even when we're sleeping, zoning out, or pretending to listen during Zoom meetings.

Brain imaging techniques like fMRI and PET scans have revealed that different regions of the brain handle different tasks, from movement and language to memory and emotion.
Sure, not every neuron fires simultaneously (that would be chaos — and probably a seizure), but that doesn't mean those neurons are lazy. They take turns, like a perfectly choreographed dance of electrical activity.
If We Only Used 10%, We'd Be in Trouble!
Let's think practically. The human brain uses about 20% of your body's total energy, even though it makes up only about 2% of your body weight. If 90% of it were just sitting there twiddling its metaphorical thumbs, evolution would have given us smaller, cheaper brains. But it didn't. Why? Because every neuron matters — whether it's helping you compose a symphony or just remember where you left your keys.

The Real “Unlocking” Happens in Learning
So no, there isn't a hidden brain reserve waiting to be awakened by meditation, magic pills, or alien technology. The real “unused potential” lies not in our neurons, but in how we challenge them. Every time you learn a new skill, solve a problem, or even read an article debunking this myth (hi there 👋), you're literally strengthening connections in your brain.

Your Brain
A Full-Time WORKER
As Scientific American put it, “We use virtually every part of the brain, and most of the brain is active almost all the time.” Translation: your brain is not holding out on you. Even the act of you reading this sentence involves multiple regions firing in sync. If only 10% worked, you wouldn't be reading—you'd be drooling on the couch.
